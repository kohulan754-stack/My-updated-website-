A PERSONAL REFLECTION

Why Listening Is
More Powerful
Than Speaking.

Sometimes, the most meaningful thing we can contribute to a conversation is not another opinion, but our attention.

BY A KOHULAN • LEARNING / MINDSET

I chose this topic because I see myself as a good listener. I have realised that listening is one of the ways I learn, grow and get exposed to things I might never have discovered on my own.

More than just hearing words

There is a difference between hearing someone and actually listening to them. Hearing can happen without much effort. Listening requires attention. It means giving someone the space to explain what they think, what they have experienced and why they see something differently.

When I listen carefully, I am not only collecting words. I am trying to understand the thought behind them. Sometimes a small point in a conversation can make me question what I already know or look at a familiar subject from a new angle.

Listening exposes us to a bigger world

Every person has a different set of experiences. Their knowledge, mistakes, interests and way of looking at life are not exactly the same as mine. By listening, I get a chance to learn from those differences without having to experience every situation myself.

This is what I find powerful about listening: it takes me beyond my own point of view. A conversation can introduce me to an idea, a field, a problem or an experience that I had not even thought about before. I may not agree with everything I hear, but I can still learn something from understanding why another person thinks that way.

Listening does not mean accepting every opinion. It means being open enough to understand it before deciding what you think.

Understanding before responding

In many conversations, people are eager to prepare their reply while the other person is still speaking. We sometimes focus more on what we want to say than on what is actually being said. When that happens, a conversation can become a competition to speak instead of an opportunity to understand.

I believe listening first helps me respond with more thought. It gives me time to consider the information, notice what I may have missed and ask better questions. I do not have to respond immediately to every point. A considered response is often more useful than a quick one.

What it means in my learning journey

As a student exploring Computer Science and technology, I know that I still have a lot to learn. I cannot depend only on what I already know. Listening to teachers, classmates, people with experience and even people who approach a problem differently can introduce me to methods and ideas I would not have found by myself.

For me, learning is not limited to a classroom, a textbook or a screen. It can also happen through a conversation. Someone explaining how they solved a problem, describing a mistake they made or sharing an unfamiliar perspective can give me a new direction to explore.

Listening and speaking need each other

I do not think speaking is unimportant. Sharing our thoughts, asking questions and expressing ideas are all necessary. But speaking becomes more meaningful when it is supported by listening. If I only speak, I keep repeating what I already know. When I listen, I make room to learn something new; when I speak after that, I can contribute with a better understanding.

Being a good listener does not mean staying silent all the time. It means knowing when to listen, when to ask and when to share. The balance matters.

A habit I want to keep

I want to continue being someone who listens with curiosity. Not because I have nothing to say, but because I know there is always something I may not know yet. Every conversation will not change my thinking, and every person will not have an answer for me. Still, approaching people with attention gives me the chance to discover something useful.

To me, listening is powerful because it keeps learning open. It reminds me that my perspective is only one among many, and that growth often begins when I make room for someone else's.

ONE THOUGHT TO TAKE AWAY

Speak to express what you know. Listen to discover what you don't.
